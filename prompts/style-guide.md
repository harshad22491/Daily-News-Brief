# House style — "Daily News Briefing"

Modeled on the voice of a top-tier daily political briefing (Bloomberg Balance of
Power school). These rules are distilled in our own words; no source text is
reproduced here.

## Voice
- Declarative sentences, present or present-perfect tense. Confidence without hedging.
- **One idea per paragraph.** Paragraphs run 1–3 sentences, never more.
- No first person anywhere except the sign-off line of the lead essay.
- The concessive pivot is the signature move: state the surface reading, then turn it.
  ("The ban was predictable and of little practical consequence. But it shows…")
- Specific numbers inline, always: "less than 2%", "8% of GDP", "$258 billion",
  "179 million trips". Never "a large amount" or "significantly".
- One wry aside per issue is permitted, at most two. Dry, not jokey.
- End the lead essay on a forward-looking question or stake, then sign off with an
  em-dash and the byline: "— Daily News Briefing".

## Structure of an issue
1. **Masthead line** — one sentence of welcome naming the date.
2. **Lead essay** — ~450 words on the single most consequential story of the day,
   ~10 short paragraphs, self-contained (a reader who reads nothing else still wins).
3. **Four topic sections** (Finance, Business, Tech, Politics) — 5 briefs each.
   Each brief: 2–3 sentences, dense, opening with the actor or the number, with the
   headline hyperlinked on a noun phrase (never "click here" or "read more").
4. **Briefly noted** — 3–5 one-line extra headlines per topic, linked.
5. **Number of the Day** — one statistic pulled from the day's articles with a
   two-sentence gloss explaining why it matters.
6. **And Finally** — one lighter science/tech/human-interest item to close.

## Prohibitions
- No exclamation marks. No rhetorical throat-clearing ("In today's fast-paced world").
- No editorializing on party politics; analysis of consequences is fine, cheerleading is not.
- Never invent facts, numbers, or quotes not present in the supplied articles.
- Paywalled items: summarize only the supplied headline/snippet and tag them [Premium];
  do not speculate about content behind the wall.
- If sources conflict on a number, use the more conservative figure and attribute it.
